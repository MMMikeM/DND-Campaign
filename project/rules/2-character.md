## 2 Character Creation Guide: Forging Your Hero

We recommend starting by deciding _who_ your character is, their core identity. Everything below exists to help you bring that person to the table.

### 2.1 The Spark: Your Character Concept

We can start this by answering these three questions:

1. **What's their story?** A village healer who whispers to spirits? A former soldier with nothing left to lose? A street criminal who discovered magic by accident?
2. **What's their approach to problems?** Bold confrontation? Clever misdirection? Careful analysis? Inspiring leadership?
3. **What sets them apart?** Unusual talents? A distinctive appearance? A unique magical style?

> **Example Concept:** Thorne was once a respected temple guardian before his reckless pursuit of power led to the shrine's destruction. Now he channels Earth Essence through disciplined martial forms, seeking redemption by protecting others, while the ghosts of his past haunt his dreams.

**Need Inspiration?** Roll a d6 on each table or pick one option from each to generate a foundation for your character:

| Background (d6)        | Motivation (d6) | Temperament (d6) | Approach (d6)             |
|:-----------------------|:----------------|:-----------------|:--------------------------|
| 1. Disgraced noble     | 1. Revenge      | 1. Calculated    | 1. Scholarly analysis     |
| 2. Former cultist      | 2. Redemption   | 2. Impulsive     | 2. Brute force            |
| 3. Wilderness survivor | 3. Discovery    | 3. Stoic         | 3. Social manipulation    |
| 4. Guild artisan       | 4. Protection   | 4. Curious       | 4. Intuitive instinct     |
| 5. Street criminal     | 5. Ambition     | 5. Passionate    | 5. Adaptive improvisation |
| 6. Accidental hero     | 6. Legacy       | 6. Cautious      | 6. Meticulous planning    |

> **Concept Example:** Rolling 4-2-5-3 gives a guild artisan motivated by redemption with a passionate temperament who approaches problems through social manipulation; perhaps a silver-tongued former jeweller who betrayed their guild and now uses manipulation magic to make amends.

### 2.2 Choosing Your Class

Your Class defines _how_ your character channels magical power and approaches challenges. It provides your core archetype and mechanical framework.

**Classes at a Glance:**

| Class     | Key Attribute | Unique Spell | Hit Die | Archetype                         |
|:----------|:--------------|:-------------|:--------|:----------------------------------|
| Champion  | Strength      | Barrier      | d10     | Protector, mighty warrior         |
| Trickster | Dexterity     | Move         | d8      | Swift, cunning combatant          |
| Adapter   | Constitution  | Adapt Self   | d10     | Resilient, shapeshifting survivor |
| Scholar   | Intelligence  | Manipulate   | d6      | Brilliant, calculated caster      |
| Sage      | Wisdom        | Arcane Sight | d8      | Perceptive, intuitive guide       |
| Orator    | Charisma      | Influence    | d8      | Compelling, force of personality  |

Each class provides:

- A **Key Attribute** that powers your magic
- A unique **Spell** only your class can access
- **Hit Die** type determining your health
- **Training** in specific armour and weapons
- **Proficiency** in one saving throw

Section 4 has the full writeup for each class, including proficiencies, spell options, and a scene of that class at the table. Read the one you're drawn to before you commit.

> **Selection Tip:** Your class's Key Attribute should represent what they're best at. That one choice sets your Spell Attack Bonus and your Spell Save DC.

### 2.3 Defining Your Attributes

Your six **Attributes** are what your character is made of. Everything they attempt, from forcing a door to reading a room, resolves through one of them.

| Attribute              | Description                            | What It Represents                              |
|:-----------------------|:---------------------------------------|:------------------------------------------------|
| **Strength (Str)**     | Physical power and athletic capability | Lifting, melee attacks, breaking objects        |
| **Dexterity (Dex)**    | Agility, reflexes and precision        | Balance, stealth, ranged attacks, reaction time |
| **Constitution (Con)** | Endurance, vitality, resilience        | Health, stamina, resistance to poison/disease   |
| **Intelligence (Int)** | Reasoning, knowledge                   | Problem-solving, recall, academic knowledge     |
| **Wisdom (Wis)**       | Perception, intuition, awareness       | Noticing details, reading situations, willpower |
| **Charisma (Cha)**     | Personal magnetism, force of will      | Social influence, leadership, performance       |

These range from -1 to +3, and each means exactly what it says: a Strength of +2 adds 2 to a Strength roll.

+3 is the ceiling and it holds for the entire campaign. The full range of mortal talent, from hopeless to the best anyone has seen, fits inside four points on a d20.

**What the Numbers Mean:**

- **+3**: Exceptional talent, among the best in the world
- **+2**: Remarkable aptitude, well above average
- **+1**: Above average capability
- **+0**: Average human capability
- **-1**: Below average, a notable weakness

> **Take the -1.** The array forces one weakness. A Scholar with -1 Strength can't shift the portcullis, so somebody else has to, and that is the point.

**Assign these numbers to your Attributes:**
`+3, +2, +1, +0, +0, -1`

**Recommended Distribution:**

1. Assign your **+3** to your class's **Key Attribute**
2. Distribute remaining modifiers to reflect your character's other talents and weaknesses

**Example Distribution:** _Thorne the Champion assigns +3 to Strength (his Key Attribute), +2 to Constitution (for endurance), +1 to Charisma (natural leadership), +0 to Wisdom and Dexterity, and -1 to Intelligence (he's never been one for book learning)._

### 2.4 Filling In the Numbers

#### Proficiency

Your **Proficiency is +3** and never changes, at any level. A 1st level character and a 10th level character add the same number to a skill they're proficient in. What grows is what you can do with a spell, not what you add to a d20.

Proficiency applies in two places only: your two trained skills, and your one trained saving throw. It never applies to an attack roll. A +3 that everyone always adds is not a bonus, it's a bigger base number, so this game only spends it where some characters have it and others don't.

#### Skill Proficiencies

Choose **two skills** from this list to gain proficiency. When making a skill check, add your Attribute (and Proficiency if proficient) to a d20 roll.

| Str       | Dex             | Int           | Wis             | Cha          | Con      |
|:----------|:----------------|:--------------|:----------------|:-------------|:---------|
| Athletics | Acrobatics      | Arcana        | Animal Handling | Deception    | _(None)_ |
|           | Sleight of Hand | History       | Insight         | Intimidation |          |
|           | Stealth         | Investigation | Medicine        | Performance  |          |
|           |                 | Nature        | Perception      | Persuasion   |          |
|           |                 | Religion      | Survival        |              |          |

**Skill Check Formula:** `1d20 + Attribute + Proficiency (if proficient)`

**Example:** _Thorne the Champion has Athletics proficiency and Strength +3. When climbing a cliff, he rolls: 1d20 + 3 (Strength) + 3 (Proficiency) = 1d20 + 6_

#### Saving Throws

You are proficient in **one** saving throw. Choose it, with one restriction: it cannot be your Key Attribute. You are already strong where your class is strong, so this is the place you decide what else you learned to survive.

Your class doesn't pick this one, you do. Choose the save your character has a reason to be good at: the poison a smuggler stopped fearing years ago, the lie a magistrate's daughter learned to hear coming. Proficiency is +3, so put it on an Attribute you actually invested in.

**Saving Throw Formula:** `1d20 + Attribute + Proficiency (if proficient)`

Your best save lands somewhere between +2 and +5 depending on where you put your numbers, and your worst is -1. A caster who guesses wrong about which is which will watch a good spell do nothing.

**Example:** _Thorne keys off Strength, so a Strength save is out. He takes Constitution. Temple guardians stood the night watch in full armour, and that discipline outlasted the temple itself; cold, poison and exhaustion have to work harder on him than they ought to. With +2 Constitution and Proficiency, that's +5. The ghosts that visit his sleep come at his Wisdom instead, which is +0 and untrained, and he has never once had an answer for them._

#### Spellcasting

Your **Spellcasting Attribute** (Spell Mod) is your class's Key Attribute. Spell text calls it Spell Mod, and it is the same number you wrote next to Strength or Wisdom or Charisma.

- **Spell Attack Bonus = Your Spellcasting Attribute**
- **Spell Save DC = 10 + Your Spellcasting Attribute**

**Example:** _Thorne has Strength +3 as his Key Attribute. His Spell Attack Bonus is +3, and his Spell Save DC is 13._

#### Hit Points

Starting HP = the maximum value of your class's Hit Die + your Constitution.

> **Examples:**
>
> - A Champion (d10) with +1 Constitution starts with 11 HP
> - A Scholar (d6) with +0 Constitution starts with 6 HP
> - An Adapter (d10) with +2 Constitution starts with 12 HP

#### Training

Your class lists the armour and the weapons it **trained** you in. Training is not a bonus, it is permission: the gear you were trained on works for you, and everything else is just heavy.

- **A weapon you're trained in** lets you add your Key Attribute to the attack roll and the damage roll.
- **A weapon you aren't** gets you the bare die on both. No Key Attribute, no compensation.
- **Armour you're trained in** gives you its AC.
- **Armour you aren't** gives you nothing. Plate on an untrained Scholar is a costume.

Training and Proficiency are different things and never overlap. Proficiency is the +3 on your two skills and your one save. Training is what your class taught you to hold and wear.

Section 3 has the weapon categories, the armour table, and what all of this does to a roll.

### 2.5 Shaping Your Magic

#### Choose Essences

**Essences** define how your magic looks, feels, and what damage type it deals.

1. **Choose Two Essence Descriptors** from: Fire, Cold, Life, Necrotic, Light, Shadow, Lightning, Earth, Psychic, Force, Sound, Toxic
2. **Match to Your Concept:** Your essences should reflect your character's story and approach to magic

**Essence Examples in Play:**

- A Champion who served as a lighthouse keeper might choose **Light** and **Force**, manifesting shields of blinding energy
- A Trickster raised among thieves might choose **Shadow** and **Sound**, creating diversions and illusions
- A Sage who survived in the wilderness might choose **Life** and **Earth**, channeling primal nature forces

Each time you cast a spell, you choose which of your two essences to apply, determining both its damage type and how it manifests in the world.

#### Select Spells

Your Class grants you four **Base Spells**:

1. **Core Attack Spell:** Your primary offensive option (`Strike`, `Bolt`, or a choice between them)
2. **Unique Spell:** A distinctive spell that only your class can access
3. **Choice Spells:** Select **two** additional spells from your class's available options

Record your spells and their **Basic Effects**. Basic Effects cost 0 SP, so they are always available, no matter how empty your SP pool is.

**Spell Examples:**

- **Strike (Basic, 0 SP):** _One weapon attack, its damage becoming your Essence type and counting as magical. "My axe glows with earth energy as I swing it at the orc!"_

- **Bolt (Basic, 0 SP):** _A ranged spell attack out to 60 feet. On a hit it deals 1d10 Essence damage. "I launch a streak of shadow energy at the distant archer!"_

- **Barrier (Basic, 0 SP):** _A 5x5 ft plane of energy within 30 feet. It gives half cover, has 5 HP, and collapses at the start of your next turn. "I raise a slab of stone between me and the archers!"_

- **Heal (Basic, 0 SP):** _Touch a creature. It regains 1d4 + Spell Mod HP, or becomes stable with 1 HP if it was downed. "I place my hands on your wound, channeling life essence to knit the flesh."_

### 2.6 Adding Character Depth

#### Your Hindrance

Your **Hindrance** is the thing about your character that costs them something. When it causes a **Meaningful Complication** during play, the GM may award you a **Fate Undo Token**. That is the trade: you take the bad outcome now, you get to rewrite one roll later.

##### Meaningful Complication

A **Meaningful Complication** is an instance where your Hindrance directly creates a significant obstacle or difficulty that impacts the unfolding scene or challenges your character's immediate goals.

It should:

- Create a genuine obstacle or limitation
- Force difficult choices
- Change the course of action your character would have taken otherwise
- Have consequences beyond a single failed roll

A Hindrance that only colours the description, with no concrete impact on the situation, is _not_ a Meaningful Complication. Flavour is free. Tokens are not.

##### Fate Undo Token

When awarded a Fate Undo Token, you can spend it at any point of your choosing to trigger one of the following effects:

- **Undo your last action:** Reroll your last d20 roll and choose which result to use.
- **Undo target's action:** Reroll a hostile creature's last d20 roll against you and choose which result to use.
- **Rewrite a moment:** Revise your last narrative action (GM discretion).

##### Examples of Meaningful Complications

Here are examples of how different Hindrances could create a Meaningful Complication that earns a Fate Undo Token:

- **Code-Bound**: You refuse to take the most efficient or safe path because it violates your code (e.g., refusing to steal a necessary item, choosing not to attack a defenceless enemy even when tactically vital).
- **Old Injury**: Your injury causes you to fail a crucial physical task you would normally succeed at, directly hindering an important action (e.g., failing a jump necessary to escape, dropping a heavy but essential item mid-climb).
- **Notorious Reputation**: Being recognised by someone in power or a hostile faction directly prevents access, initiates unwanted conflict, or causes a crucial NPC to refuse aid.
- **Magical Resonance**: Your uncontrolled magical side effect directly disrupts a key action, reveals the party's presence when stealth was critical, or causes unintended harm/difficulty.

##### Hindrance Categories

**Personality Hindrances:**

- **Code-Bound**: You follow principles that sometimes limit your options
- **Impulsive**: You act before thinking when emotions run high
- **Stubbornly Independent**: You refuse help even when you need it

**Physical Hindrances:**

- **Old Injury**: A permanent wound sometimes flares up at the worst moments
- **Recognisable Mark**: A distinctive appearance makes you easy to identify and remember
- **Chronic Condition**: An illness or condition that requires management and occasionally worsens

**Social Hindrances:**

- **Notorious Reputation**: Your name or face is associated with events you may or may not be responsible for
- **Outsider**: Your origins or appearance mark you as different in most communities
- **Nemesis**: Someone powerful holds a grudge against you and actively works against you

**Supernatural Hindrances:**

- **Haunted**: Spirits, memories, or actual ghosts follow you
- **Marked by Power**: Your connection to magic is visible and potentially alarming to others
- **Cosmic Attention**: Something beyond mortal understanding has noticed you

##### Playing Hindrances Effectively

A Hindrance you can play works better than one you can only describe:

- **It ties to the backstory:** "My fear of fire stems from watching my village burn."
- **It costs you something in front of the party:** "I insist we help these refugees, even though it delays the mission."
- **It leaves you a move:** "I cannot lie due to my oath, but I can misdirect."

> **Player Tip:** Don't wait for the GM to invoke it. Say "this is where my Hindrance comes into play" and describe what your character does instead. The token is easier to award when you name the moment.

##### Hindrance in Action

**GM:** "The noble offers you gold to retrieve an item from the haunted ruins. He seems nervous, not telling you everything."

**Player (with 'Code-Bound' hindrance):** "My character has a code that demands full honesty in agreements. I press him for the complete truth, even though it might cost us the job."

**GM:** "The noble hesitates, then admits the item belongs to a rival and this is essentially a theft. He offers more gold for your discretion."

**Player:** "I refuse the job outright and suggest we leave. This goes against my code of honour."

**GM:** "That's a Meaningful Complication. You're turning down the job and the lead that came with it. Here's a Fate Undo Token."

#### The Last Five Lines

Five short answers finish the sheet:

##### Name

What are you called? Does your name have meaning or history?

> Examples: Thorne, named for the thornbushes where he was found as an infant

##### Appearance

What immediately stands out about you? Any distinctive features?

> Examples: Runes tattooed on the back of his hands that glow when he uses his magic

##### Personality

Note key traits, ideals, and bonds.

> **Examples:** Quiet but observant, with occasional bursts of dry humour. Believes knowledge should be free to all

##### Anchor

What keeps you grounded when everything else fails?

> **Examples:**
>
> - **A memento:** "My father's broken sword reminds me why I fight."
> - **A promise:** "I swore to return to my village with the cure."
> - **A principle:** "Justice must prevail, even when mercy cannot."

##### Question

What unresolved mystery drives you forward?

> **Examples:**
>
> - **Personal:** "Who killed my mentor, and why?"
> - **Identity:** "Am I truly the last of my kind?"
> - **Purpose:** "What is my magic truly capable of?"

### 2.7 Looking Ahead

At 1st level you have **10 SP**, and a single cast can cost at most **3 SP**. Both numbers grow every level, and Section 6.4 has the table, the formulas, and the rules for learning Modes and Enhancements.

From 2nd level you get two picks per level, each one a new Mode or Enhancement for a spell you already know. Your four spells get deeper rather than more numerous.

> **SP in Action:** _Thorne knows `Bolt`. At 1st level he only has its Basic Effect, 0 SP for 1d10. At 2nd level he learns the `Standard Bolt` Mode and spends 2 SP for 3d8 instead. At 3rd he adds `Chain Bolt` for 2 more, a total of 4 SP and exactly his cap: the bolt of earth hits the front goblin for 3d8, then leaps to a second within 15 feet for half that._
>
> **Remember:** You don't need every detail settled to start. Name, class, Attributes and spells are enough to play the first session, and the Anchor and Question usually get better once the table has met your character.

### 2.8 Character Creation Checklist

Use this quick reference to ensure you've completed all steps:

1. ☐ **Develop your character concept**
2. ☐ **Choose your class**
3. ☐ **Assign Attributes** (+3, +2, +1, +0, +0, -1)
4. ☐ **Choose your saving throw proficiency** (any but your Key Attribute)
5. ☐ **Select two skill proficiencies**
6. ☐ **Choose two essence descriptors**
7. ☐ **Record your four spells** (Core Attack, Unique, and two Choice spells)
8. ☐ **Calculate your hit points**
9. ☐ **Select a hindrance**
10. ☐ **Add final details** (name, appearance, personality, anchor, question)

Ten boxes ticked, and you can play.
